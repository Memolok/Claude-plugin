# Working alone

Memolok is usually described in team language, which makes it sound like something for organizations with
governance committees. It was designed for single practitioners too, deliberately, because people working
alone are not immune to either failure mode it addresses.

## You are a team with yourself

The first decisions in a solo greenfield project are the easiest and most pleasant ones you will make.
The whole context is in your head at once: the goal, the materials, the tools, your own skills, a rough
sketch of how to get there. It feels effortless, and it feels unnecessary to write any of it down.

Two weeks later you are looking at the consequences with no idea why you did it that way. Did you have a
plan for the pipe in that wall, or did you get lucky? Was there a reason for glue instead of screws, or
was that just the first thing you thought of? Should the schema really have been denormalized there?

For every practical purpose, the person who made that decision is a different person from the one asking.
You are working in a team with past-you and future-you, and neither of them answers questions.

## Cognitive overload does the same thing faster

Even inside one long working session, the same wall arrives. You cannot hold a medium-sized project in
your head at once. Most decisions cluster tightly, so moving from one to the next feels organic — until
this decision depends on something you settled a dozen steps ago, and you have to swap that context back
in. Add two or three more, and holding all of it simultaneously is simply not possible. You need the
relevant slice of each of those interlocking decisions, not the whole thing.

Think of it as hats. The you wearing the carpenter hat has a different working context from the you
wearing the planning hat or the interior-design hat. Context switching fragments you into several
collaborators just as effectively as hiring people would.

## What changes

Nothing about the methodology. A weekend project accrues decision debt on the same terms a merger does —
smaller amounts, same compounding. What changes is the formality: solo work skips the governance
ceremony entirely, records at the point of commitment, and moves on.

The value shows up the first time you ask "why on earth did I do that" and the answer is sitting there.
