# Why you have to say what you expect

At the moment of commitment you are asked for one more thing beyond the choice and the reasoning: what
you expect to happen as a result. This is the part practitioners push back on hardest, and it is the part
that makes everything else worth doing.

## A bet, not a hope

Committing to a course of action *is* placing an informed bet on the future. Stating the expected outcome
makes the bet explicit: *we expect authentication latency to drop by thirty percent*. Not "we expect
improvement" — something a later observation could actually contradict.

A decision with no stated expectation cannot be wrong. That sounds comfortable and is precisely the
problem: it also cannot be right, cannot be learned from, and cannot be assessed by anyone, including you.
It is an opinion with a date on it.

## The honest package

Expectations are not just the good part. A well-formed commitment names four kinds of consequence, and
naming only the first is the most common way records go dishonest:

- **What you expect to gain** — the benefit you are buying.
- **What you expect it to cost** — the price you are knowingly accepting.
- **What might go wrong** — the downside of the path you chose, acknowledged up front.
- **What this forces you to do next** — work the decision creates whether you like it or not.

*"We accept the operational complexity, because the throughput gain is non-negotiable, and we will need to
hire two people who understand it"* is a complete commitment. *"We chose the fast one"* is not.

Writing the costs and risks down at the moment of commitment is what stops the retrospective argument
about whether anyone saw it coming. Somebody did. It is right there.

## What it buys you

When the results arrive, they can be checked against the commitment rather than against memory. That
comparison is the entire learning mechanism — and it only exists if something was promised. A record with
no stated expectations produces nothing to compare, so the decision can never teach anyone anything,
including its own author.

There is a discipline point here too. Expectations are recorded at commitment and not adjusted afterwards
to match what happened. A tail quietly rewritten to fit the results is the most tempting and most
destructive edit available, because the record still looks complete.
