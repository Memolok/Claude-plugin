# What counts as a decision

A decision is the act, anchored in time, of moving from ambiguity to commitment. It is the moment
somebody says: *given what we know now, we commit to this course of action, to satisfy this specific
need.*

That definition does real work, because it disqualifies things that feel like decisions and rescues
things that do not.

## No commitment, no decision

A four-hour meeting where everyone debated constructively, three good options were proposed, and the room
agreed the second one looked strongest — that meeting produced no decision. It produced deliberation.
Deliberation is valuable and worth recording, but until somebody commits, nothing has been decided and
nothing is in force.

This is why recording deliberation and recording a commitment are two separate acts. Conflating them
produces a ledger full of decisions nobody actually made, which is worse than an empty one.

## Situations with "no choice" are still choices

The mirror error is more interesting. When new regulation lands, companies typically comply, and it feels
like there was nothing to decide. But compliance was chosen. The organization could have closed the
affected line of business. It could have exited the market. It could have shut down entirely. It could
have chosen strategic non-compliance and budgeted for the penalty.

Those alternatives were real, they were rejected, and the reasons they were rejected are exactly what a
future reader needs — especially the reader wondering, three years later, why the company is carrying a
compliance function that costs more than the market it protects.

Recording forced-looking decisions is not bureaucratic theatre. It is where a lot of the most expensive
reasoning hides.

## Opinionated, and not

Memolok is strict about **how you record** a decision: what has to be present, when it seals, what may
never be rewritten afterwards.

It is completely neutral about **how you make** one. Consensus, strict hierarchy, committee vote,
individual mandate, a coin toss you are honest about — all fine, all recordable. Memolok is not a
governance model and will not tell you how to run your organization.

It is equally neutral about domain. Software examples are convenient, not definitional; legal, medical,
sales, aerospace, and home renovation decisions all have the same fundamental shape.
