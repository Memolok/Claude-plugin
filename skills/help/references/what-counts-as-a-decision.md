# What counts as a decision

A decision is the act, anchored in time, of moving from ambiguity to commitment. It is the moment somebody says: *given what we know now, we commit to this course of action, to satisfy this specific need.*

That definition does real work, because it disqualifies things that feel like decisions and rescues things that do not.

## No commitment, no decision

A four-hour meeting where everyone debated constructively, three good options were proposed, and the room agreed the second one looked strongest — that meeting produced no decision. It produced deliberation. Deliberation is valuable, and worth recording when what it was about will last; that said, until somebody commits, nothing has been decided and nothing is in force.

This is why recording deliberation and recording a commitment are two separate acts. Conflating them produces a ledger full of decisions nobody actually made, which is worse than an empty one.

## Situations with "no choice" are still choices

The mirror error is more interesting. When new regulation lands, companies typically comply, and it feels like there was nothing to decide. But compliance was chosen. The organization could have closed the affected line of business. It could have exited the market. It could have shut down entirely. It could have chosen strategic non-compliance and budgeted for the penalty.

Those alternatives were real, they were rejected, and the reasons they were rejected are exactly what a future reader needs — especially the reader wondering, three years later, why the company is carrying a compliance function that costs more than the market it protects.

Recording forced-looking decisions is not bureaucratic theatre. It is where a lot of the most expensive reasoning hides.

## Not every choice earns a record

Choosing is not enough. A choice earns a record when the outcome lasts, and real alternatives were weighed, however small the matter: it changes something people will live with, or its reasoning is worth finding again.

Weighing how to *carry out* a plan is different. Whether to migrate a database table by table or over one weekend was real deliberation, yet once the migration is done only the migrated database remains. There is nothing left to be confused about, so nothing to record. By contrast, whether to move databases at all, or how to restructure the schema everyone will build on, is long lasting: the result stays in the world, and so does the question of why.

## Opinionated, and not

Memolok is strict about **how you record** a decision: what has to be present, when it seals, what may never be rewritten afterwards.

It is completely neutral about **how you make** one. Consensus, strict hierarchy, committee vote, individual mandate, a coin toss you are honest about — all fine, all recordable. Memolok is not a governance model and will not tell you how to run your organization.

It is equally neutral about domain. Software examples are convenient, not definitional; legal, medical, sales, aerospace, and home renovation decisions all have the same fundamental shape.
